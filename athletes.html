Athletes & guardians

The record is about
the athlete.

An organisation operates Athlytica, but the athlete is the subject of every entry and the long-term owner of their own sporting history.

01 — What is kept

What an athlete's record actually contains.

Sessions, in the order they happened

With the organisation and discipline each one belonged to.

Attendance, recorded at the time

Not reconstructed afterwards from memory.

Observations with their source

Who recorded them, when, and under which protocol where one applies.

The coach's note

What to work on next, in the coach's own words, attributed to them.

02 — What is deliberately absent

No score. No ranking. No comparison to another child.

These are not features waiting to be built. They are decisions.

No composite score

Nothing blends a skating session with a football one into a single number about a child.

No ranking or percentile

Athlytica does not rank children or score them against each other.

No public athlete page

The controlled pilot does not propose one, and a record is not a profile.

No placement service

Athlytica does not place athletes with clubs or scholarship programmes. It keeps the record of what they did.

03 — Guardians

Access for a family starts with the organisation.

Where the athlete is a child, family access depends on an invitation and on recorded authority — not on asking.

Invitation-only

Access starts with the participating organisation. There is no public sign-up for a child's record.

Recorded authority

Access depends on documented authority for that athlete.

Release-dependent

Availability follows the current product and privacy process, and remains under controlled validation.

Minimum necessary

An initial enquiry asks for adult contact details only — never a child's name, health information, images or performance data.

Guardian access is invitation-only and remains under controlled validation. Availability depends on the participating organisation, recorded authority and the current product release.

04 — Why it is worth keeping

The person who knew what you could do in March may not be coaching you in September.

The record leaves with the coach

What a coach knew may stop being accessible the moment the coaching relationship changes.

One athlete, several files

A child who skates on Tuesday and plays football on Saturday collects a new file at every door. None is complete, and no two agree.

A number with no origin

A score written down without who recorded it, when, and against which protocol cannot be checked later. In five years it is a number in a column.

The goal is a durable record the athlete and family can access over time, subject to permissions, the participating organisation's role and applicable law.
