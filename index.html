01 — The problem

Training happens.
Then the notebook leaves.

Coaches change. Programmes end. A family moves clubs, or schools, or cities. Every one of those is normal — and every one of them currently erases years of an athlete’s development, because the record lived in somebody’s notebook, somebody’s phone, or a spreadsheet nobody can find.

The record leaves with the coach

The person who knows what an athlete could do in March is often not the person coaching them in September. What they knew may no longer be accessible when the coaching relationship changes.

The goal is a durable record the athlete and family can access over time, subject to permissions, the participating organisation's role and applicable law.

One athlete, several disconnected records

A child who skates on Tuesday and plays football on Saturday collects a new file at every door. None of them is complete, and no two agree.

The identity model is designed to connect an athlete's authorized records without blending separate organisations or disciplines.

A number with no origin

A score written down without who recorded it, when, and against which versioned operator protocol, where one applies, cannot be checked later. In five years it is a number in a column and nothing more.

Provenance is what makes a record still worth something later.

02 — Evidence

Recorded, coach-attested and
independently verified are different states.

Athlytica keeps what was recorded separate from what a coach attested and what has been independently verified, with the state shown on each entry. That distinction keeps the source clear over time.

Recorded

A measurement exists, with a timestamp, a session and an author. Everything in the system today is at least this.

Source, session, timestamp and author kept together

Coach-attested

An identified coach, affiliated to a named organization, stands behind it. Strong — and not the same thing as independent confirmation.

Who recorded it, when, and under which organization

Independently verified

Confirmed by someone other than the person who recorded it. Nothing in Athlytica carries this state yet, and the product will not pretend otherwise. When it does, the method will be published.

Not claimed, because it is not true yet

What this is not

There is no composite score, no ranking, no percentile and no seal. Metrics stay attached to their organization and discipline; nothing blends a skating session with a football one into a single number about a child.

A record, not a verdict
Session context — time, venue, roster and completion — accumulates as evidence. No single signal is treated as proof on its own.

04 — The coach workflow

Start the record. Coach the session.
Finish the entry.

The intended pilot workflow is to open the session when coaching starts, coach normally, then return during or immediately after the session to complete the entry, review what is blank and close the record before leaving.

01 — Open the session

At the start, pick the organization and discipline. A server-generated reference is issued and the roster freezes at that moment.

02 — Attendance

Mark during the session or immediately afterwards while the session remains open. Unmarked is never silently read as absent — a blank is a blank.

03 — Assessment

Against the selected versioned operator protocol.

04 — Coach note

What the athlete should work on next, in the coach’s own words, attributed to them.

05 — Review

What was captured and what is still blank, shown before anything closes.

06 — Finish

Terminal. A closed session takes no new evidence, which is what makes the register worth reading later.

For academies & independent coaches

Record infrastructure for athlete development

Everything the coaching already does — enrolment, registers, assessment, reporting to parents — in one place, with the evidence kept beside each record instead of in a notebook.

  • Sessions and rostersFrozen when the session starts, so a register cannot be rewritten afterwards
  • Independent practiceYou do not need an academy behind you — declare a practice and record under it
  • More than one disciplineEach stays in its own context; nothing is averaged across sports
