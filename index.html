Athlete record infrastructure · Nairobi

The athlete record
built to last.

Athlytica keeps recorded and coach-attested athlete entries with their source and context. Independent verification is a separate state and is not currently claimed.

What it is

Record infrastructure for athlete development. Not a dashboard, not a scouting service, and not a league table — the thing underneath those, which is a register of what an athlete actually did, with the evidence kept beside it.

Who uses it now

Academies and independent coaches running controlled pilots in Nairobi. They are the operational users. The athlete is the subject of the record, and the long-term owner of their own sporting history.

The problem it solves

Coaches change, programmes end, families move. Each of those is normal, and each currently erases years of development because the record lived in a notebook, a phone, or a spreadsheet nobody can find.

Why the records can be trusted

Because every entry carries who recorded it, when, under which organisation and against which protocol — and because the state of each entry is shown rather than flattened into one word. How evidence is handled.

01 — The system

From a measurement to a decision, without losing the origin.

Each step depends on the one before it. Skip a step and everything downstream is an assertion rather than a finding.

Data→ Evidence→ Verification→ Intelligence→ Decision

Data

A measurement or observation is captured during a session, against a versioned protocol where one applies.

Evidence

It is kept with its source, session, time and author, so it can be checked later rather than merely remembered.

Verification

A separate state, reached only when someone other than the recorder confirms it. Not currently claimed.

Intelligence

What the accumulated record supports — always attached to its context and its limitations, never a single number about a child.

Decision

A coach, an academy or a family deciding what to do next, able to see what the decision rests on.

02 — Evidence states

Recorded, coach-attested and independently verified are different things.

Collapsing them into one word is how a record stops being worth reading. Athlytica shows the state on each entry, and the state is a word, not a colour.

Recorded

A measurement exists

Captured against a protocol, with its source, session, time and author. Everything in the system today is at least this.

Coach-attested

A named coach stands behind it

An identified coach, affiliated to a named organisation. Strong — and not the same thing as independent confirmation.

Independently verified

Checked by someone else

Nothing in Athlytica carries this state yet, and the product will not pretend otherwise. When it does, the method will be published.

Pending

Submitted, not yet checked

The value is shown, not hidden. A reader can see what was claimed while it is still being checked.

Disputed

Challenged, and still shown

The value stands and is displayed with the challenge beside it. A system that hides disputed numbers can be pressured into hiding inconvenient ones.

Sample

Not a real record

Example data used to explain the system. Always labelled, never dismissible.

03 — The record

One record, and everything it carries.

Not a report card and not a PDF. A living record with provenance attached, so that in five years somebody can still tell where each number came from.

What this is not

There is no composite score, no ranking, no percentile and no seal. Metrics stay attached to their organisation and discipline; nothing blends a skating session with a football one into a single number about a child.

One session, one open record

Opening a session creates its reference and freezes the roster. A closed session refuses new evidence, which is what makes the register worth reading later.

Every number has an origin

Who entered it, when, under which organisation, and against which versioned protocol where one applies. That is what makes a record checkable rather than merely old.
