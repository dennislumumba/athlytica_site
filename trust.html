Trust & evidence

A record is only worth
what you can check.

Athlytica's only real asset is that its records mean what they say. That is easier to lose than to build, so the rules below are stated in public rather than kept internally.

01 — The six states

Every entry carries its state, and the state is a word.

Colour never carries status on its own. Desaturate this page and every state still reads, because each one is a word on a surface with a rule above it.

Recorded

A measurement exists

Captured against a protocol, with its source, session, time and author. Everything in the system today is at least this.

Coach-attested

A named coach stands behind it

An identified coach, affiliated to a named organisation. Strong — and not the same thing as independent confirmation.

Independently verified

Checked by someone else

Nothing in Athlytica carries this state yet, and the product will not pretend otherwise. When it does, the method will be published.

Pending

Submitted, not yet checked

The value is shown, not hidden. A reader can see what was claimed while it is still being checked.

Disputed

Challenged, and still shown

The value stands and is displayed with the challenge beside it. A system that hides disputed numbers can be pressured into hiding inconvenient ones.

Sample

Not a real record

Example data used to explain the system. Always labelled, never dismissible.

02 — Provenance

Who recorded it, when, and under what.

Provenance is what makes a record still worth something later. It is kept with the entry, not in a separate log that can drift away from it.

03 — What is not claimed

The limitations are published, not buried.

These are the current limits of what Athlytica can support. They are listed because a reader deserves them before they rely on anything.

No independent verification

Nothing in Athlytica currently carries the independently-verified state. When something does, the method will be published first.

No reliability statistic

Inter-rater reliability is not merely unmeasured; with one coach in production it is unmeasurable. Saying otherwise would be the exact failure this design exists to prevent.

No comparison group

No percentile, ranking, population norm or age-normalised benchmark, because the derivation protocol for them has not been written.

Ordinal, not interval

A 2/4 assessment scale is an ordered judgement, not a measurement you can average.

Coach-observed capture

Most entries are what a coach observed in a session, attributed to them — not instrument output.

No AI prediction

Athlytica makes no automated prediction about an athlete's future, and does not describe itself as doing so.

If a sample cannot support a comparison, Athlytica shows the limitation instead of the chart. A panel reading "insufficient comparison group" is a better asset than an impressive chart built on nothing.

04 — Data handling

The principles the record is kept under.

Purpose before collection

What a coaching decision requires, and the reason stated before the asking.

Evidence before intelligence

Nothing is inferred from a record that the record cannot support.

Provenance before trust

An entry without an origin is not treated as evidence.

Context before comparison

An observation belongs to its session, organisation and discipline, and is not compared out of them.

Permission before sharing

Guardian access is invitation-only and depends on recorded authority.

Minimum necessary access

An initial enquiry takes adult contact details and nothing about a child.

05 — In practice

Where the infrastructure is currently used.

Big Ice, a skating academy in Nairobi, uses Athlytica to keep session, attendance and coach-attested assessment records where a programme enables it. It is one deployment inside the same portfolio, and it is named here because a concrete example is more useful than an abstraction — not as evidence of broad adoption, which would be a different claim and one Athlytica cannot currently make.

Big Ice develops athletes. Athlytica records, structures and verifies evidence. NRHL organises competition. Three separate organisations with three separate brands.
